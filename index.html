01 — THE IDEA

Your finances should tell you
what's coming.

Most finance tools are good at showing where your money went. BaselineLab’s center of gravity is the future: recurring bills and income, planned events, account-level cash flow, and early shortfall warnings.

Your data lives on your device. When you choose to use iCloud, it syncs privately between your Mac and iPad through your own Apple account—not through BaselineLab servers.

05 — QUESTIONS, ANSWERED

Clear answers
before you buy.

The full illustrated user manual ships with BaselineLab — and you can read it now.

What does it run on?

BaselineLab is for Mac and iPad (macOS 15+, iPadOS 18+), with private iCloud sync between them. There is no iPhone version at launch.

Does it connect to my bank?

No — by design. You import the statement files your bank already provides (QFX, OFX, QBO, or CSV). That means no bank passwords shared, no third-party aggregator sitting between you and your accounts, and nothing that breaks when a bank changes its login page.

Which file formats does it read?

QFX, OFX, QBO, and CSV — every bank and card issuer offers at least one of these from its statement or activity page. Imports are previewed row by row, with duplicate detection, before anything is saved.

Is it a subscription?

No. Buy it once, use it. No account to create, no monthly fee.

Where is my financial data stored?

On your device. If you sign in to iCloud, it syncs privately through your Apple account between your Mac and iPad — BaselineLab operates no servers and receives none of your data. You can export everything (CSVs plus a full snapshot) at any time.

How is this different from a budgeting app?

Budgeting apps look backward at categories. BaselineLab looks forward at cash: it projects every account day by day, models how your accounts cover each other, and warns you — with a date and a dollar amount — when your plan won't cover what's coming.
